Chapter 1 · Functions and Models Full lessons →

§1.1–1.6 · the language the rest of the course uses

A function turns each input into exactly one output. Chapter 1 is the toolbox: reading graphs, finding domains, building new functions from old ones, and undoing functions with inverses and logs.

Function and domain

Each \(x\) gives exactly one \(f(x)\). A curve is a function if every vertical line hits it at most once.

\(\tfrac1{g}\): \(g\ne0\) · \(\sqrt{g}\): \(g\ge0\) · \(\ln g\): \(g>0\)

Even and odd

\(f(-x)=f(x)\) even · \(f(-x)=-f(x)\) odd

Even is symmetric about the y-axis (\(x^2,\cos x\)). Odd is symmetric about the origin (\(x^3,\sin x\)).

Essential functions

Linear \(mx+b\) · polynomial · power \(x^a\) · rational \(\tfrac{P}{Q}\) · trig · exponential \(a^x\) · logarithmic \(\log_a x\).

Transformations

\(y=a\,f\big(b(x-h)\big)+k\)

\(h\): right, \(k\): up, \(a\): vertical stretch (\(a<0\) flips over the x-axis), \(b\): horizontal squeeze by \(b\). Changes inside work backwards.

Composition

\((f\circ g)(x)=f\big(g(x)\big)\)

Work inside out. The domain is the \(x\) in the domain of \(g\) with \(g(x)\) in the domain of \(f\). Usually \(f\circ g\ne g\circ f\).

Exponentials

\(a^{x+y}=a^xa^y\) \((a^x)^y=a^{xy}\) \(a^{-x}=\tfrac1{a^x}\)

\(a^x>0\) always. \(e\approx2.71828\) is the base whose tangent at \(x=0\) has slope 1.

Inverse functions

\(f^{-1}(y)=x \iff f(x)=y\)

Only one-to-one functions have inverses (horizontal line test). To find one: write \(y=f(x)\), solve for \(x\), swap. The graph is the mirror image in \(y=x\), and \(f(f^{-1}(x))=x\).

Logarithms

\(\log_a x=y\iff a^y=x\) \(\ln x=\log_e x\)

\(\ln(xy)=\ln x+\ln y\) \(\ln\tfrac xy=\ln x-\ln y\) \(\ln x^r=r\ln x\)

\(\ln e^x=x\) \(e^{\ln x}=x\) \(\log_a x=\tfrac{\ln x}{\ln a}\)

Inverse trig

\(\arcsin x\): \([-1,1]\to[-\tfrac\pi2,\tfrac\pi2]\) · \(\arctan x\): \(\mathbb R\to(-\tfrac\pi2,\tfrac\pi2)\)

\(\sin x\) has to be restricted before it can be inverted.

Mistakes that cost marks

  • \(f(x-3)\) moves the graph right, not left.
  • \(\ln(x+y)\ne\ln x+\ln y\).
  • \(f^{-1}(x)\) is the inverse, not \(\tfrac1{f(x)}\).

On last year's paper

No question of its own, but every question uses it: logs in Q2(2) and Q5, composition in every chain rule.

Chapter 2 · Limits and Derivatives Full lessons →

§2.1–2.8 · about 30% of last year's marks

A limit asks what \(f(x)\) approaches as \(x\) gets close to \(a\), never what happens at \(a\). Limits define continuity, and the limit of secant slopes defines the derivative.

Limit and one-sided limits

\(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to a}f(x)=L\)

\(f(x)\) gets close to \(L\) as \(x\to a\), \(x\ne a\). The limit exists only when \(\lim_{x\to a^-}=\lim_{x\to a^+}\).

Limit laws

Limits of sums, products, constant multiples, powers and roots are the sums, products, etc. of the limits. Quotients too, if the bottom limit \(\ne0\).

Direct substitution works for polynomials, rationals, trig, exp and log at points in their domain.

When you get 0/0

0/0 means more work, not "undefined":

  • Factor and cancel: \(\tfrac{x^2-5x+6}{x-2}=x-3\)
  • Conjugate for roots: multiply by \(\sqrt{\cdot}+k\)
  • Common denominator for fractions

Trig limits and squeeze

\(\displaystyle\lim_{\theta\to0}\frac{\sin\theta}{\theta}=1\) \(\lim_{\theta\to0}\frac{\cos\theta-1}{\theta}=0\)

Squeeze: if \(g\le f\le h\) near \(a\) and \(g,h\to L\), then \(f\to L\). Example: \(x^2\cos\tfrac1x\to0\).

Precise definition (2.4)

\(\forall\varepsilon>0\ \exists\delta>0:\ 0<|x-a|<\delta\Rightarrow|f(x)-L|<\varepsilon\)

"You can make \(f(x)\) as close to \(L\) as you want by taking \(x\) close enough to \(a\)."

Continuity

\(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to a}f(x)=f(a)\)

Three checks: \(f(a)\) is defined, the limit exists, and they are equal. Failures: removable (hole), jump, infinite. Sums, products, quotients and compositions of continuous functions are continuous.

Intermediate Value Theorem

If \(f\) is continuous on \([a,b]\) and \(N\) is between \(f(a)\) and \(f(b)\), then \(f(c)=N\) for some \(c\in(a,b)\).

Use it to prove an equation has a root: find a sign change.

Limits at infinity

\(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac1{x^r}=0\) \(\lim_{x\to-\infty}e^x=0\)

For rational functions compare the top and bottom degrees: equal gives the ratio of leading coefficients, top smaller gives 0, top bigger gives \(\pm\infty\). That limit \(L\) is a horizontal asymptote \(y=L\).

The derivative

\(\displaystyle f'(a)=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{f(a+h)-f(a)}{h}=\lim_{x\to a}\frac{f(x)-f(a)}{x-a}\)

It is the slope of the tangent and the instantaneous rate of change. Tangent line: \(y-f(a)=f'(a)(x-a)\).

Differentiable vs continuous

differentiable \(\Rightarrow\) continuous \(\Rightarrow\) limit exists

Not the other way: \(|x|\) is continuous at 0 but not differentiable (corner). A derivative also fails at cusps, vertical tangents and breaks.

Mistakes that cost marks

  • Stopping at 0/0. Factor, rationalize or simplify.
  • Assuming the limit equals \(f(a)\). That's only true if \(f\) is continuous.
  • Proving continuity without checking both sides of a piecewise function.
  • Using the IVT without saying "\(f\) is continuous on \([a,b]\)".

Chapter 3 · Differentiation Rules Full lessons →

§3.1–3.11 · about 35% of last year's marks

Instead of using the limit definition every time, you build any derivative from a few rules. The chain rule is the one that appears everywhere.

Basic rules

\((c)'=0\) \((x^n)'=nx^{n-1}\) \((cf)'=cf'\) \((f\pm g)'=f'\pm g'\)

\((e^x)'=e^x\) \((a^x)'=a^x\ln a\)

Product and quotient

\((fg)'=f'g+fg'\)

\(\left(\dfrac fg\right)'=\dfrac{f'g-fg'}{g^2}\)

Quotient: "low d-high minus high d-low, over low squared".

Trig derivatives

\((\sin x)'=\cos x\) \((\cos x)'=-\sin x\) \((\tan x)'=\sec^2x\)

\((\sec x)'=\sec x\tan x\) \((\csc x)'=-\csc x\cot x\) \((\cot x)'=-\csc^2x\)

Derivatives of \(\sin\) and \(\cos\) repeat every 4: use \(n \bmod 4\) for the \(n\)th derivative.

Chain rule

\(\big(f(g(x))\big)'=f'\big(g(x)\big)\cdot g'(x)\) \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=\dfrac{dy}{du}\dfrac{du}{dx}\)

\((u^n)'=nu^{n-1}u'\) \((e^u)'=e^uu'\) \((\sin u)'=\cos u\cdot u'\)

Outside first, keep the inside, then multiply by the inside's derivative.

Implicit differentiation

Differentiate both sides with respect to \(x\), treating \(y\) as a function of \(x\): \((y^3)'=3y^2y'\), \((xy)'=y+xy'\). Then collect the \(y'\) terms and solve.

\((\arcsin x)'=\dfrac1{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\) \((\arctan x)'=\dfrac1{1+x^2}\)

Log derivatives

\((\ln x)'=\dfrac1x\) \((\ln|x|)'=\dfrac1x\) \((\ln u)'=\dfrac{u'}u\) \((\log_ax)'=\dfrac1{x\ln a}\)

Logarithmic differentiation for \(f(x)^{g(x)}\) or messy products: take \(\ln\), differentiate, multiply by \(y\). Example: \(y=x^x\Rightarrow y'=x^x(\ln x+1)\).

Rates and growth (3.7–3.8)

\(v=s'(t)\) \(a=v'(t)=s''(t)\)

\(\dfrac{dy}{dt}=ky\ \Rightarrow\ y=y_0e^{kt}\)

\(k>0\) growth, \(k<0\) decay. Half-life: \(t=\tfrac{\ln2}{|k|}\).

Related rates (3.9)

  1. Draw it, name the variables
  2. Write an equation linking them
  3. Differentiate with respect to \(t\)
  4. Substitute the numbers after differentiating

Linear approximation (3.10–3.11)

\(L(x)=f(a)+f'(a)(x-a)\) \(dy=f'(x)\,dx\)

\(\sinh x=\tfrac{e^x-e^{-x}}2\) \(\cosh x=\tfrac{e^x+e^{-x}}2\) \((\sinh)'=\cosh\ \ (\cosh)'=\sinh\)

Mistakes that cost marks

  • Forgetting the inner derivative in the chain rule.
  • \((fg)'\ne f'g'\) and \(\left(\tfrac fg\right)'\ne\tfrac{f'}{g'}\).
  • Treating \(x^x\) like \(x^n\): you need logarithmic differentiation.
  • In implicit differentiation, forgetting \(y'\) on terms with \(y\).

Chapter 4 · Applications of Differentiation Full lessons →

§4.1–4.9 · about 35% of last year's marks

The sign of \(f'\) tells you where a graph rises and falls; the sign of \(f''\) tells you how it bends. That is enough to find maxima and minima, sketch curves and solve optimization problems.

Extrema and critical numbers

Extreme Value Theorem: \(f\) continuous on a closed \([a,b]\) has an absolute max and min there.

Critical number: \(c\) in the domain with \(f'(c)=0\) or \(f'(c)\) undefined. Local extrema happen only at critical numbers (Fermat).

Closed interval method

  1. Find the critical numbers inside \((a,b)\)
  2. Evaluate \(f\) at them and at \(a\), \(b\)
  3. Largest value is the absolute max, smallest the absolute min

Rolle and the Mean Value Theorem

Conditions: \(f\) continuous on \([a,b]\) and differentiable on \((a,b)\).

MVT: \(f'(c)=\dfrac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}\) for some \(c\in(a,b)\)

Rolle is the case \(f(a)=f(b)\Rightarrow f'(c)=0\). Consequence: \(f'=0\) everywhere means \(f\) is constant.

Increasing / decreasing

\(f'>0\Rightarrow\) increasing \(\) \(f'<0\Rightarrow\) decreasing

First derivative test: \(f'\) changes \(+\to-\): local max; \(-\to+\): local min; no change: neither. Make a sign chart.

Concavity

\(f''>0\) concave up ∪ \(\) \(f''<0\) concave down ∩

Inflection point: where concavity changes. Second derivative test: \(f'(c)=0\) and \(f''(c)>0\) is a min, \(f''(c)<0\) a max, \(f''(c)=0\) tells you nothing.

L'Hôpital's rule (4.4)

\(\dfrac00\) or \(\dfrac\infty\infty:\) \(\displaystyle\lim\frac fg=\lim\frac{f'}{g'}\)

Other forms: \(0\cdot\infty\) → rewrite as a fraction; \(\infty-\infty\) → common denominator; \(1^\infty,0^0,\infty^0\) → take \(\ln\) first.

Curve sketching (4.5)

Domain → intercepts → symmetry → asymptotes → increasing/decreasing → local max/min → concavity and inflection → sketch.

Optimization (4.7)

  1. Draw it, name the variables
  2. Write the quantity to optimize
  3. Use the constraint to get one variable
  4. Find critical points; check endpoints or use the first derivative test

Newton and antiderivatives (4.8–4.9)

\(x_{n+1}=x_n-\dfrac{f(x_n)}{f'(x_n)}\)

\(\int x^n=\tfrac{x^{n+1}}{n+1}+C\ (n\ne-1)\) \(\int\tfrac1x=\ln|x|+C\) \(\int e^x=e^x+C\)

\(\int\cos x=\sin x+C\) \(\int\sin x=-\cos x+C\) \(\int\sec^2x=\tan x+C\)

Mistakes that cost marks

  • Forgetting to check the endpoints in the closed interval method.
  • Thinking \(f'(c)=0\) guarantees an extremum: \(x^3\) at 0.
  • Using L'Hôpital when the limit isn't 0/0 or ∞/∞.
  • Using the quotient rule in L'Hôpital. Differentiate top and bottom separately.
  • Writing the MVT conclusion without checking both conditions.

How to learn math visually

Seven methods, ranked by evidence

Pictures help most when they are tied tightly to the words and symbols, so every method here links a picture to a step you actually do.

Sketch before you solve

Words and pictures are processed in two channels. Students given a picture with the explanation solved more new problems than students given text alone, as long as the picture matched the words.

Do it today: draw a rough graph for every exam problem before you calculate anything.

Mayer & Moreno (2002); Paivio's dual coding theory

Worked example, then fade it

Studying a full solution beats solving cold when a topic is new. Then remove the last step, then two steps, until you solve alone. In math, worked examples have a medium effect on performance.

Do it today: read a solution, cover its last line, and finish it yourself.

Renkl et al. (2002); math meta-analysis, g ≈ 0.48 (Educational Psychology Review, 2023)

Test yourself

Practice testing got the top "high utility" rating in a review of ten common study techniques. Rereading and highlighting scored low.

Do it today: make Anki cards for every derivative rule and the theorem conditions.

Dunlosky et al. (2013)

Space it out

Spreading study over several days was the other top-rated technique. Three short sessions on three days beat one long night.

Do it today: touch every chapter on at least two different days before Nov 11.

Dunlosky et al. (2013)

Mix problem types

In a randomized trial with 787 students, the group that mixed problem types scored 61% on a surprise test a month later, against 38% for the group that practised one type at a time.

Do it today: do one mixed set with limits, derivatives and max/min together, like the real exam.

Rohrer, Dedrick, Hartwig & Cheung (2020), Journal of Educational Psychology

Explain every step

Saying why each step works improves both procedures and understanding, with small to moderate effects in math. It works best on worked examples.

Do it today: explain one solution out loud as if you were teaching your AI class.

Rittle-Johnson, Loehr & Durkin (2017), ZDM; Chi's self-explanation research

Keep a mistake log

A practical habit that turns errors into retrieval practice: write down every mistake and redo it two days later without looking.

Do it today: start a page called "mistakes" and add to it after every practice set.

Applies practice testing and spacing to your own errors

A note on "visual learners"

The idea that people learn best when teaching matches their style (visual, auditory, and so on) has almost no experimental support. A major review found the needed evidence was missing, and several good studies contradicted it. What does work is pairing clear pictures with words and symbols. That helps everyone, so this site uses visuals because they work, not because of a learner label.

Pashler, McDaniel, Rohrer & Bjork (2008), Psychological Science in the Public Interest

Transformations

§1.3 · book p.36 · PDF p.68 · Ex. 1–3, 9–16

What to notice: h and k move the graph, but a negative h inside the bracket moves it left. Changes inside the function act on x, so they work backwards.

Inverse = mirror across y = x

§1.6 · book p.58 · PDF p.90 · Ex. 3–8, 21–26

\( f(x)=e^x \quad\leftrightarrow\quad f^{-1}(x)=\ln x \)

What to notice: the point \((a, e^a)\) on \(e^x\) always has a twin \((e^a, a)\) on \(\ln x\). Swapping x and y is the same as reflecting in the dashed line.

A limit at a hole Exam Q4(1)

§2.2–2.3 · book p.87 · PDF p.119 · Ex. 2.3: 1–32

\( f(x)=\dfrac{x^2-5x+6}{x-2} \)

What to notice: f is not defined at 2 (the open circle), but from both sides the values close in on one number. A limit cares about near 2, never at 2.

Show steps

\( \dfrac{x^2-5x+6}{x-2}=\dfrac{(x-2)(x-3)}{x-2}=x-3 \) for \(x\ne2\), so the limit is \(2-3=-1\).

Continuity of a piecewise function Exam Q3

§2.5 · book p.118 · PDF p.150

\( f(x)=\begin{cases}x-1 & x<0\\ c & x=0\\ \cos(\pi-x) & x>0\end{cases} \)

What to notice: continuity needs three things to agree: the left limit, the right limit and the actual value. Move c away from −1 and the dot jumps off the curve.

Show steps

Left: \(\lim_{x\to0^-}(x-1)=-1\). Right: \(\lim_{x\to0^+}\cos(\pi-x)=\cos\pi=-1\). Value: \(f(0)=-1\) on the exam. All three are equal, so f is continuous at 0.

Secant becomes tangent

§2.1 & 2.7 · book p.82 · PDF p.114 · Ex. 2.1: 3–5

\( f(x)=x^2,\ a=1 \)

What to notice: the secant slope is \(2+h\). As h shrinks the secant turns into the tangent, and the slope settles at 2. That limit is the derivative.

The squeeze theorem Exam Q4(3)

§2.3 · book p.99 · PDF p.131 · Ex. 37–48

\( -x^2\le x^2\cos\!\left(\tfrac1x\right)\le x^2 \)

What to notice: \(\cos(1/x)\) never settles down, but it is trapped between −1 and 1. Multiplied by \(x^2\), it is squeezed between two parabolas that both go to 0.

Show steps

Since \(-1\le\cos(1/x)\le1\), we get \(-x^2\le x^2\cos(1/x)\le x^2\). Both \(\pm x^2\to0\), so by the squeeze theorem the limit is 0.

f and f′ side by side

§2.8 & 3.1 · book p.154 · PDF p.186

Tip: drag on the top graph.

What to notice: the height of the bottom graph equals the slope of the top one. Where f has a peak or a valley, f′ crosses zero.

Chain rule as linked rates

§3.4 · book p.198 · PDF p.230 · Ex. 1–30

\( y=\sin(x^2),\quad \dfrac{dy}{dx}=\cos(x^2)\cdot 2x \)

What to notice: the inner part \(u=x^2\) changes at rate \(2x\). The outer part \(\sin u\) changes at rate \(\cos u\). The total rate is the product, so the outer derivative is evaluated at the inner function.

Implicit curve and its tangent Exam Q6

§3.5 · book p.209 · PDF p.241 · Ex. 5–22, 25–30

\( x^2+2y^3=3xy \)

What to notice: this curve fails the vertical line test, so y is not one function of x. Implicit differentiation still gives the slope at any single point on it.

Show steps

Differentiate both sides: \(2x+6y^2y'=3y+3xy'\). Solve: \(y'=\dfrac{3y-2x}{6y^2-3x}\). At \((1,1)\): \(y'=\dfrac{3-2}{6-3}=\dfrac13\). Tangent: \(y-1=\tfrac13(x-1)\), or \(y=\tfrac13x+\tfrac23\).

Absolute max and min on [0, 2] Exam Q7

§4.1 · book p.274 · PDF p.306

\( f(x)=\dfrac{x^2+3}{1+x} \)

What to notice: on a closed interval the answer is always one of a short list: the endpoints and the critical points inside. Check each one; don't guess from the picture.

Show steps

\(f'(x)=\dfrac{2x(1+x)-(x^2+3)}{(1+x)^2}=\dfrac{(x+3)(x-1)}{(1+x)^2}\), so the critical point in \([0,2]\) is \(x=1\). Values: \(f(0)=3,\ f(1)=2,\ f(2)=\tfrac73\). Absolute max 3 at \(x=0\); absolute min 2 at \(x=1\).

Mean Value Theorem Exam Q1(2)

§4.2 · book p.284 · PDF p.316

\( f(x)=x^3-2x^2+x \)

What to notice: for any a and b, at least one tangent (orange) is parallel to the secant. Some point inside has \(f'(c)\) equal to the average slope.

Show steps

Exam Q1(2) option B: f(0)=10, f(8)=30 means average slope \(\tfrac{30-10}{8-0}=2.5\), so the MVT promises \(f'(c)=2.5\), not 2. Option A is the Intermediate Value Theorem: 25 is between 10 and 30, so it must be true.

Increasing, decreasing, extrema Exam Q8

§4.3 · book p.290 · PDF p.322

\( f(x)=x^3-2x^2+x \)
\( f'(x)=(3x-1)(x-1) \)

f′ > 0 ↗1/3f′ < 0 ↘1f′ > 0 ↗

What to notice: the color changes exactly where f′ changes sign. Going up then down gives a local max; going down then up gives a local min.

Show steps

Critical points: \(x=\tfrac13\) and \(x=1\). Increasing on \((-\infty,\tfrac13)\) and \((1,\infty)\), decreasing on \((\tfrac13,1)\). Local max \(f(\tfrac13)=\tfrac{4}{27}\); local min \(f(1)=0\).

How this site is designed

The layout is one long page with a sticky top bar, because a student revising wants to move between strategies, graphs and the plan without losing their place. A thin progress line under the bar shows how far down the page you are. On phones the links fold into a menu button. The order follows a study session: why a method works, then the graphs to practise on, then the plan, then extra resources.
